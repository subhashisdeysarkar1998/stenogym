Exam Strategy & FAQs

Why is General Awareness (GK & Current Affairs) considered the true rank-decider? +
In exams like SSC Stenographer, almost every serious aspirant achieves high, comparable scores in English and Reasoning. General Awareness accounts for 50 critical marks. Because there are no formulas to guess answers, it has the widest score variance. That massive 25-mark swing is exactly what decides whether you get your preferred Ministry posting or miss the cutoff entirely.
How does daily GK practice improve scores compared to last-minute cramming? +
GA is the only section where you can attempt 50 questions in under 10 minutes, banking crucial extra time for lengthy English passages. Last-minute cramming leads to heavy negative marking due to confusion between similar dates. Daily 10-minute micro-learning with Stenogym's active MCQ drills turns factual recall into second nature.
When is the Current Affairs section updated every day? +
Our AI engine pulls, filters, and formats the latest national and global news automatically at 6:00 AM every single morning. This ensures that early-morning studiers always have access to the freshest, exam-relevant capsule to start their day.
How are full and half mistakes calculated in the Steno Skill Test? +
Full mistakes include omitted words, substituted words, and additions. Half mistakes include spelling errors, capitalization mistakes, and missing punctuation. Stenogym's AI grading replicates this exact SSC formula so you always know your true score.
What is the difference in dictation speed for SSC Grade C and Grade D? +
For SSC Grade D, the dictation speed is 80 Words Per Minute (WPM) for 10 minutes (800 words total). For SSC Grade C, the speed is 100 WPM for 10 minutes (1000 words total).
How much time is given for reading the shorthand notes before typing? +
Usually, candidates are given 10 minutes to read their shorthand outlines before they are required to start typing the transcript on the computer. This reading period is crucial for mental preparation.
Are commas and full stops dictated in the actual exams? +
It varies by state, but traditionally High Court and SSC exams do dictate "Full Stop" and "Para". Commas are generally NOT dictated; you are expected to place them logically based on grammar.
